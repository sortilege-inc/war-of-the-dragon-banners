Llwyd is a name few people can say, so his friends call him Paun: an old word for the peacock, which
is where the bird gets its name. He is the best-looking of the squires, and people use the name partly in
fun. He takes it the better way, as the bird of immortality.

He is the son of Sir Geraint ap Llwyd, who lost an eye at the River Parrot, made his name at Lincoln, and died
for the rebels at the Battle of Kent. His uncle held the family's lands until the Saxons killed him at the
Night of the Long Knives. Paun is due to inherit, if anyone is ever in a position to confirm it. His family's
hunting has been cursed by the fair folk since his father offended them, and he is looking for a way round it.

He is quiet, composed, careful, and honourable, and he plays the harp and sings. He composed the Lay of
Squire's Hill and has performed it more than once; Branwen was in the audience the first time. He would like
the bards to remember him for driving back the Saxons.

His arms are gold, with a sun, and a bend of dark blue-green.
