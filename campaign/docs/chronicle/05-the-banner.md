---
title: The Banner
part: Winter 464 to Easter 465 · sessions played January to March 2026, not recorded
---

These sessions were played but not recorded. What is set down here is what Paun's own record lists for
464 and 465, and what the table recalled of them afterwards.

The story proper begins in the winter of 464, the year after the Night of the Long Knives, with a miserable
Christmas at Vagon, where the Wolves and their squires wintered with Sir Elad. None of them had a lord.
The Earl of Salisbury had gone to Ebbsfleet and not come back, and his heir, the boy Roderick, was
somewhere no one could say.

In 465 the Wolves and their squires rescued a young man from an attack by bandits. He was dark-haired,
thin, younger than the squires, and his name was Merlin. He had dreams, and so, it turned out,
did some of the squires, Drust most of all. There was a banner to be found and carried to the one who
should bear it before winter came; once it was found, Merlin said, the man would reveal himself. Merlin knew
roughly where it lay: north, where three kingdoms meet. The Wolves agreed to go.

Paun's record for the year lists, besides the rescue: the Battle of Squire's Hill; the first performance of
his Lay of Squire's Hill, with Branwen in the audience; a fight in which they killed barguests, the great
black hounds; a charge on the Saxons at Bambury; and the Easter feast in Cameliard.

In Cameliard two brothers were at odds. The younger, Martin, the legitimate son, the squires met in the
field, fighting Saxons. The elder, Rhodri, who is not, held the castle and the lordship, and the Wolves kept
Easter at his table at Carohaise. Martin had made his brother's forces out to be smaller than they were.
Neither had the men to beat the other, and both, like everyone, were against the Saxons.

The squires served at the feast. Paun performed the Lay of Squire's Hill again. Sineda had the attention of
two serving girls, twins, and nothing came of it. And Drust learned what the Wolves most wanted to know: that young Roderick of Salisbury, only lately made a squire, had been squired to one of the
knights of Cameliard.
